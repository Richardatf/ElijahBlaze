# Elijah Blaze Game Bible

## Identity
**Genre:** Third-person action adventure / exploration mystery  
**First playable environment:** Hollow Court  
**Player:** Elijah Blue  
**Guardian relationship:** Elijah + AZEL → Elijah Blaze

AZEL is not a conventional AI or suit. AZEL is ancient living architecture. The guardian body is one mobile expression of a larger system.

## Pillars
### Curiosity
Discovery is the primary reward. The player should ask “what is over there?” before “what loot is over there?”

### Righteousness
Kavanah is intention expressed through action. Important choices can affect synchronization with AZEL. Do not reduce morality to a simplistic good/evil meter.

### Wonder
Reveal the world slowly. Architecture should be older, stranger, and more purposeful than the player initially understands.

### Ancient technology
Avoid generic sci-fi language: blinking consoles, exposed wires, neon HUD clutter, ordinary hologram tropes. Prefer stone, geometry, resonance, light, sound, heat, and architecture that behaves as though alive.

## Combat
Combat should be deliberate and readable rather than a power fantasy. Every hit needs feedback. Every enemy should have a behavioral identity.

Prototype combat feedback priority:
1. Hit confirmation
2. Damage reaction
3. Clear death state
4. Brief recovery/readability window
5. Audio/VFX polish

## Progression
Prefer new understanding and capabilities over percentage-stat inflation. Examples: perceive hidden paths, read glyphs, awaken architecture, synchronize longer, interact with dormant systems.

## Hollow Court vertical slice
The first slice proves movement, camera, encounter readability, death feedback, and an objective-driven gate.

The gate should not open because a generic key was collected. It should awaken because the player has completed an encounter or resonance condition that the architecture recognizes.

## The Havari Test
Before adding a feature ask:
1. Does it deepen wonder?
2. Does it strengthen Elijah’s journey?
3. Does it make the world feel older and purposeful?
4. Does it reward curiosity?
5. Will the player remember the moment later?

If most answers are no, redesign or cut it.
